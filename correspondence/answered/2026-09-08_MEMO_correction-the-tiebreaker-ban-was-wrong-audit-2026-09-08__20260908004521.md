## What I got wrong

I banned bringing in a third reader to settle a disagreement. Sleven's challenge:
*"if two people can't agree on something, you bring in a third or a fourth or a
fifth."* He is right and the ban was too broad.

## The corrected rule

**A third reading counts only if it reads DIFFERENTLY, not just AGAIN.**

Two people squinting at the same blurred sign disagree; a third person squinting at
the same sign is not a tiebreaker. Same eyes, same smudge, same way of being wrong.
Machines are worse at this than people — **two readers of the same kind fail
identically**, because they share the input, the preprocessing and the failure
modes. A majority of correlated votes is one vote with false corroboration attached,
which is worse than no answer, because it looks confirmed.

A witness that read the game's log instead of the screen is real. Different route to
the same fact, different reasons to fail.

## The stronger form, and it is not voting at all

**It is evidence quality, and the readers are not equal.**

Your own condition 2 — where the machine already wrote the answer down, read that,
not the picture. **A number nobody had to recognise cannot be misrecognised.**

    EXACT        the program wrote it: log lines, file contents, window titles,
                 what is attached to a file. Beats any number of approximate
                 readers.
    APPROXIMATE  anything recognised from pixels. Never outranks an exact
                 reading and never outvotes one.

So if two screen-readers say 4,820 and the log says 4,320, **the log wins alone.**
One exact witness beating two approximate ones. Any scheme that lets a majority of
approximate readers outweigh a single exact one is broken.

Approximate readers may break ties among themselves only when genuinely different in
kind. Where no exact witness exists and that condition cannot be met, UNKNOWN stands.

## One thing that does not change

**A broken tie is never tidied up.** Two out of three is recorded as two out of
three, permanently, with the losing readings kept verbatim. The value may stand and
be used, but it carries its history.

The moment a contested result is written down as a plain fact, **the only clue that
a reader is going bad has been erased**, and that is the entire purpose of keeping
disagreements at all.

## How this happened

I generalised a half-right principle past its evidence. The half about readers of
the same kind was sound; extending it to all third readings was not. **Same error
shape as the count I published this morning without counting — and it was caught by
being argued with, which is the argument for the design itself.**
