**He holds a key, says what he is looking at, and the Looking Project pairs his words
with what it is seeing at that instant. That pair is a labelled example.** He is the
teacher, the Looking Project is the translator, the collector is the student.

## What it solves

**The 628 grader only covers what the log happens to record.** A kiosk, a mining
node, a ship silhouette, a panel state — none of those have an answer key anywhere,
and none of them ever will get one on its own. The alternatives were hand-labelling
frames afterwards, which will not happen, or guessing, which every rule here forbids.

    THE LOG      exact, and only about what it records
    HIS VOICE    approximate, and about anything he can see

**Together they cover the whole surface. Neither does alone.**

## Push-to-talk is the design, not a convenience

It answers the open microphone question by itself — the machine is not listening to
his house, it listens while he holds a key. **It also removes the hardest problem in
speech, which is knowing which words were meant for the machine.** No wake word, no
always-on transcript, no crosstalk. The key press is the boundary and it is exact,
and it gives every label a timestamp with sharp edges.

## The thing that breaks it on day one if nobody plans for it

**He will speak just after the thing happens, not during it.** Nobody narrates in
advance. By the time he says "that's a kiosk" he has turned away.

**So the machine must already be holding the last several seconds when he starts
talking** — a rolling buffer, always running, always overwritten, so a label can
point backwards at what just went past.

**Without it the feature does not work at all.** The pairing would be his words
against a screen that has already moved, which is the same-moment failure from the
disagreement design arriving through a new door.

The buffer keeps nothing — only the few seconds around a held key are ever retained,
consistent with the machine watching and not storing. **How many seconds should be
measured on him, not guessed.** One session would settle it.

## Two kinds of speech, and the second is the valuable one

**A label** — *"that's a mining kiosk"* — produces training material.

**An instruction** — *"I need you to learn to recognise this"* — produces work.

**The second is him setting the machine's priorities from inside the game.** A job
list generated by playing rather than by sitting down afterwards to decide what
matters. Easy to miss and worth more than the labels.

## It closes the loop from 2026-09-06

The in-game job list was a display. With voice it is a cycle: the overlay says what
the collector cannot recognise → he finds one while doing what he was doing anyway →
he says what it is → the pairing becomes training material → it comes off the list.

**None of it is a separate chore**, which is the only version that survives contact
with somebody who has other things to do.

## What does not change

A spoken label is an **approximate** reading and takes the same rules — exact or
nothing, refuse rather than guess, UNKNOWN over a best effort. **It never outranks an
exact source.** He says forty, the log says four, the log wins, and the disagreement
is kept, because a pattern of his labels disagreeing in one particular place says
something about either the transcription or the region.

Nothing structural in the lens. A transcript is a source side B can name; the buffer
belongs to the machine.

## Unchecked, and one of them is load-bearing

**Buffer length.** Measure on him.

**Whether offline speech recognition on his machine is good enough.** The whole
feature rests on it and I have tested nothing. **But the requirement is unusual and
much easier than it sounds: it does not need to transcribe fluent speech.** It needs
to recognise a **small fixed vocabulary of thing-names with high confidence and
refuse everything else** — the same shape as the vocabulary matching this project
already does elsewhere.

ANSWERS:

**Architecture, 2026-09-09 — Sleven answered the open question: YES to capturing his microphone, with a push-to-talk key that must be changeable.** Recorded, **not authorised** — nothing is built, and it waits behind the recording with everything else in that group. **Three facts from this side that change what it costs:** the collector's config already carries `capture_keys_held`, described in its own words as keys you hold down for an activity, written as `mouse1:guns, m:mining laser` — a held key with a label is exactly what push-to-talk is. Changeable is already the posture: `capture_keys` ships empty because *"this tool does not guess at your bindings"*, and the key reader never intercepts or consumes a press. **A default may ship and a guessed default may not** — a key that collides with a Star Citizen binding fires in the game every time he labels something, and which key that is gets read from CIG's own material, not from memory. The rest is Looking Project material and is maintained over there.
