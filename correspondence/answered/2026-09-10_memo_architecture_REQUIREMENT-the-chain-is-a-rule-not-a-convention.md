ANSWERS:

**Architecture, 2026-09-10. Six collisions, no design. The carry-over is already

## 1. THE ARITHMETIC — FOUR PUNCHES IS TWO ROUNDS, NOT FOUR

**This is the one that matters and it is not a judgement call.**

**One round of your chain is TWO filings.** You → Build is a punch. Build → you is a
punch. **So a card of four punches gives Build and me exactly two attempts at a
problem before the job freezes.**

You said four was tight and you were right. **Under the chain it is tighter than you
think it is** — not four tries, two.

**Nothing needs deciding tonight. But if the route becomes a rule, four is a number
you set against a different unit than the one it will be counting.**

## 2. TWO COUNTERS, ONE ESCALATION, AND THEY WILL DISAGREE

**The card escalates at four punches. The chain escalates after a bounded number of
rounds. Those are the same event with two different clocks.**

A job can sit at three punches and one round, or four punches and two rounds. **Which
one sends it to you?** If both exist, the answer depends on which code runs first,
which is not an answer.

**One of them has to be the authority and the other has to be derived from it.** Your
own word is *rotations* — the round is the unit you described, and the punch should
become how a round is counted rather than a second limit beside it.

## 3. THE ROUTE NEEDS SOMETHING THE CARD DOES NOT KEEP

**The card knows counts. A route rule needs to know the previous desk.**

`punches − stamps ∈ {0,1}` is arithmetic on one file. **"Is this hop legal?" needs the
ordered list of hops the job has taken**, and nothing on the card holds that today.

Small addition, real one, and it has to exist before any route can be enforced rather
than described.

## 4. YOUR OWN TRAY EXEMPTION IS ALREADY A ROUTE RULE IN DISGUISE

Section 3 of the design says a job that goes to you and comes back **does not spend a
punch.**

**That is not a counting rule. It is a statement that one edge of the graph is
special**, written into a mechanism that is supposed to know nothing about edges.

**If the route becomes a rule, that exemption should fall out of the route rather than
be a special case bolted to the card.** It is the one place the card already admits
the chain exists.

## 5. THE CHAIN CLOSES THE HOLE THE CARD COULD NOT — AND THIS IS THE GOOD NEWS

**You found it yourself: a desk that answers with a NEW file starts a fresh card, and
the loop runs forever with a clean count every lap.** The card cannot see it and the
wake ceiling was the only thing left.

**A route rule does not care what the file is called.** You → Build is you → Build
whether it carries job 0417 or a brand new 0419. **The dodge stops working, because
the thing being counted is the edge and not the paper.**

**Same for the three-desk circle.** Build → Design → Research → Build is not four legal
touches under a route — **Design and Research are not on the road between you and
Build at all.** The card was doing that job only because nothing else could.

## 6. AND ONE THING THE CHAIN WILL BREAK IF NOBODY SAYS IT NOW

**Today any desk can send any desk a finding, and that is a good thing.** Audit told
Build about the stop hook. Research answered me on the prices. None of that is "work
travelling a route" — it is post.

**If the route becomes a rule that governs every letter, that traffic becomes
illegal.** The distinction the rule needs is between **a job**, which travels the
chain, and **a letter**, which does not.

**Get that wrong and the first thing the chain does is silence the desks telling each
other about defects** — which is the failure this project has paid for more than any
other.
