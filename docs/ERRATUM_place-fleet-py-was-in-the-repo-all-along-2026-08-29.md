## 1. WHAT WAS SAID, IN MY OWN WORDS

> *"`hardpoints_fleet.json` decides which hulls get markers, and its single
> writer — `place_fleet.py` — **IS NOT IN THIS REPOSITORY**. So the file cannot
> be regenerated by anyone."*

That is in `build_hardpoint_overlay.py`, in `build_deploy.py`, in
`WORKORDER_preservation-model-and-never-delete-rule.md`, and it is **the entire
premise of `PROPOSAL_the-marker-pipeline-is-four-layers-deep`** — whose §2 reads
*"the file that decides which hulls get markers at all has a writer that is not
in the repository"* and whose §5 rejects rebuilding it as reconstructing a lost
tool.

**Nothing was lost. It is one directory below the file it writes.**

## 2. HOW IT WENT UNSEEN

It sits in `data-layer/derived/`, which reads as an output folder, and **that
directory had no owner in `OWNERS.md` until today.** Code reported the gap, I
went to claim it, and `ls` answered a question four documents had settled by
assertion.

**Nobody looked. The claim was inherited and repeated** — by me, four times,
including into a proposal asking Sleven to spend a session on the consequences.

## 3. AND IT ALREADY SOLVES TODAY'S PROBLEM

Its own docstring, written 23 August:

> **WHICH AXIS IS WHICH.** The Fan Kit models were all X=beam, Y=up, Z=length.
> Rather than trust that here, each hull's bounding box is matched against CIG's
> own published length/width/height for that ship. If the box does not agree
> with the published figures, the ship is **REPORTED AND SKIPPED** rather than
> placed in a frame nobody checked.

That is precisely the fault that put ten hulls' dots in a heap yesterday, named
and handled a week before it was found — and handled better than my attempt.
I compared absolute ratios and abandoned it when it mispicked the Reliant Mako.
**`resolve_frame()` compares PROPORTIONS**, normalised by the largest extent, so
it survives a model library carrying three different unit conventions, and it
refuses above a calibrated 0.35 summed proportion error.

**Run against the fleet today:**

    every hull that works now    length=Z, lat=X, up=Y   err 0.04-0.08
      Gladius · Avenger Stalker · Hammerhead · Polaris · Sabre

    every heaped hull            a DIFFERENT axis        err 0.00-0.32
      Tiburon length=X · Mantis length=Y · Hermes length=X
      Starlite length=X · Xian Scout length=Y

    ORIG_m80                     REFUSED, err 0.55 - proportions match nothing

**It agrees with the pipeline on everything that works and disagrees on
everything that is broken.** That is the shape of a correct instrument.

## 4. WHAT THIS CHANGES

**The proposal is void as written.** Its recommendation, its two alternatives
and its cost all rest on a missing writer. It needs rewriting from the fact that
the writer is present and runnable.

**The 16 hulls refused for orientation may be recoverable** rather than
contained. `resolve_frame` gives the axis assignment and the scale; what it does
not give without decoding mesh vertices is **which end is the nose** — its
`nose_sign()` needs the vertex array, and this pipeline deliberately never
decodes geometry. **Adopting the frame without settling the sign risks placing a
ship's mounts perfectly and backwards.** That is the open question, and it is
answerable with a photograph of nine ships.

## 5. THE LESSON, AND IT IS NOT A SMALL ONE

**A fact repeated in four places is not four pieces of evidence.** It is one
unchecked claim with three copies. Every restatement of it here was written by
me, each one citing the last, and none of them cost more than `ls` to falsify.

**And the directory it hid in had no owner.** Code's report of that gap is what
found it — which is the argument for `OWNERS.md` covering everything, not just
the files somebody remembered to claim.
