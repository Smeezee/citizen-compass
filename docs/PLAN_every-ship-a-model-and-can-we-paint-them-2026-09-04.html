First: I have to correct myself, and it is the important part

I told you the models kept their UV maps, so textures would drop right on. Then I came back and told you the opposite — that every model has UV zero and paint was impossible.

Both of those came from looking at one ship: the Cutlass Black. The Cutlass happens to be one of the broken ones. I generalised from a sample of one, twice, in opposite directions.

I have now measured all 234 source files and all 258 built models. The real answer is in the middle, and it is better news than the last thing I told you:

  • 178 of 234 ship source files carry real UV data.
  • 48 genuinely have none. 8 are partial.
  • Our own file conversion destroyed the UV maps that did exist. That is our bug, not RSI's. It is fixable.

2 · How we get every missing model — the four routes

Route A — Fleetyards holo mirror recommended, do this first

What it gives: 19 of the 24 missing ships, immediately.
Cost: 257 MB, one script, one afternoon. Free.
Format: 18 .gltf and 1 .glb — same family as everything we already have.
Credit: Fleetyards is a fan site re-hosting RSI's own holoviewer exports. We credit them and RSI. Nothing paid, nothing behind a wall.
Risk: Their URLs are signed blob redirects. If one has expired since August we re-query their API for a fresh one. That is a five-minute fix, not a blocker.

Route B — RSI's own ship pages

What it gives: The same files Route A gives, straight from the source instead of a mirror. Every ship page with a 3D viewer downloads one model file to your browser; we read what it downloads.
Cost: Slower. One page at a time, and RSI's viewer is JavaScript so it needs a real browser driving it, not a plain fetch.
Use it for: Backup when Fleetyards is missing or stale, and as our independent check that Fleetyards has not modified anything.

Route C — RSI Fan Kit

What it gives: The holoviewer set as a bulk download. This is how we got the 234 .ctm files we already hold.
Cost: Free, but it only refreshes when CIG updates it, so it lags new ships.
Use it for: Bulk refresh at patch boundaries, not for closing today's gap.

Route D — Your own game install the only route to real paint

What it gives: Everything. The real hulls, at full detail, with CIG's actual textures. It is the only place in the world those textures exist outside CIG.
Where it is: Data.p4k on your machine — 146 GB, and I can already reach it from here.
Cost: Real work. The archive has to be unpacked, the ship geometry converted out of CryEngine's format, and the textures converted out of DDS. That is a build project, not a script. It would also give us files far too heavy for a web page without a decimation pass.
The one thing on record you should weigh: the site currently operates under CIG's Fan Kit Agreement, which covers Fan Kit assets. Shipping unpacked game art is a different footing from shipping Fan Kit art. Rights are your call and you have already made it — I am noting the distinction as a fact, not reopening it.

What RSI publishesOpenCTM v5, MG2 — a compressed mesh format used by RSI's web holoviewer. One merged hull. No materials, no textures, no images.
What we build from itglTF 2.0 binary (.glb), compressed with KHR_draco_mesh_compression, via glTF-Transform 4.4.2.
What is inside our .glb241 of 258 have exactly one mesh and one material, named "Default", with no colour at all — just metallic 0.5 / roughness 0.5.
Textures / images0 of 258. Not one model file contains a single image. This is true at every layer including RSI's original.
Vertex data presentPosition + normal on all. A UV channel on 244 of 258. 14 have no UV channel whatsoever.
SizeTypical ship is 100k–600k vertices as one solid merged shell. No separate parts, no doors, no interior.

Proof these really are RSI's own files and not something better someone else made: the Fan Kit .ctm and our built .glb match to the exact vertex — Cutlass Black is 149,983 vertices and 265,504 triangles in both. Every community source traces back to this same upstream. There is no secret better model out there; I checked.

4 · Can we paint them — three different questions

Q1 · Can we put CIG's real ship paint on these? No.

Not from these files, and not from anywhere on the public web. A paint job is an image file, and no file in the RSI holoviewer lineage has one — not the Fan Kit, not Fleetyards, not RSI's own ship pages. The paint exists in exactly one place: inside your Data.p4k. That is Route D or nothing.

Q2 · Do the models have a UV map — the thing a texture needs to wrap onto? Yes for most sources. No for what we currently ship. And that is our fault.

A UV map is the flat pattern that says which part of a picture lands on which part of the hull. Without it, a texture has nowhere to sit. Here is what I measured, first on the 234 original files we hold:

Now the part that is on us. I decoded the UV channel inside our finished .glb files. On the Polaris — which has 1.7 MB of good UV data in the original — our built file has 89 distinct UV values across 50,000 vertices, spread over a range of ±53 million. On the Corsair and the Cutlass Black our file has exactly one distinct value. That is not a UV map, that is rubble.

The cause is the Draco compression step. Draco squeezes UV coordinates into a fixed number of steps between the smallest and largest value present. A handful of junk vertices sitting at ±50 million stretch that range so wide that every real coordinate between 0 and 1 collapses into the same bucket. We fed it good data and it came out destroyed. Clamping the junk vertices, or not Draco-compressing the UV channel, gets the maps back on those 178 ships.

Q3 · Can we make them look painted, without CIG's textures? Yes. Three ways, and two of them need no UV map at all.

  1. Triplanar projection. Instead of wrapping a picture using a UV map, the material projects it from three directions and blends. Panel lines, hull plating, wear, grime, a manufacturer colour — all land correctly on a model with zero UVs. Works on all 258 today. This is the honest workhorse.
  2. Procedural material. No image file at all. Metal, paint, edge wear and dirt generated by maths from surface angle and position. Costs nothing to download, scales to every ship, never breaks.
  3. Auto-unwrap. Generate a UV map from scratch with xatlas or Blender's smart-project, then paint whatever we like onto it. This only makes sense for a texture we author. It can never recover CIG's original layout, because that layout is only meaningful next to CIG's original texture atlases.

Worth remembering: the reason the renders stopped looking like grey clay was not geometry, it was the material. Setting metalness to 0.92, roughness to 0.34, adding an environment to reflect and recomputing the normals is what made them read as real spacecraft. Any of the three routes above builds on that, and all of them can be tuned per manufacturer — Drake grubby, Origin polished, Aegis military flat.

5 · The honest bottom line

Coverage is nearly solved. 19 of the 24 missing ships are a download away. 5 do not exist publicly in any form and never will until CIG publishes them or we pull them from your install.

CIG's real paint is off the table unless we go into Data.p4k. Everything on the public web is the same untextured holoviewer file.

But "no paint" was too strong, and I said it too confidently. Most of these hulls do carry a usable UV map — we broke it in our own pipeline. Fix that and we can put a texture on 178 ships properly rather than by projection.

And we do not need CIG's textures to stop looking like grey plastic. Triplanar or procedural material gets us hull plating, panel lines, wear and manufacturer colour on every ship including the 48 with no UVs. Nobody looking at the site will know or care that the panel lines are not CIG's exact panel lines.
