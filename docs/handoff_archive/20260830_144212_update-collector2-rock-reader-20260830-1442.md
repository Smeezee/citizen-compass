## THE 64 WAS A DOUBLE-COUNT. THE READER'S 32 IS RIGHT.

C1 reported 64 mineable-rock sightings; the reader recorded 32 and refused none.
Rather than assume either number, I measured the archive. **Each spawn line
carries the name TWICE:**

    <2026-03-27T04:51:06.328Z> Entity : 'MineableRock_SurfaceLegendary_
    Quantainium_9742042896497 - Class(MineableRock_Surf...

Once as the instance (`..._9742042896497`) and once inside `Class(...)`. So 64
mentions = **32 spawn lines = 32 sightings**, across **11 distinct real classes**.
The reader is correct and the reported figure was counting mentions.

Cross-checked from the other side: the 12 rock facts' sighting counts sum to

## THE PRICE AND PAYOUT EVIDENCE COUNTS ARE NOW DOUBLED, AND IT IS MY DOING

`-status` reads:

    mineable_rock       32        (v2 only)
    payout             624        = 312 x 2
    shop_transaction   578        = 289 x 2

I ran `-read -from` over the same 243 logs **twice** — once under `gamelog@1`
earlier today, once under `gamelog@2` now. Both readers are believed, correctly,
so `PriceRule` counts every real transaction twice and a fact that says **"from 4
sighting(s)" is four READINGS of two EVENTS.**

Nothing is corrupt and the design is behaving as written: append-only, v1 stays
believed, versions exist precisely so output can change. **But "from N sightings"
is the program's confidence signal, and re-reading one source under a new version
inflates it.** A rock seen once in one session and a rock seen once in four are
different facts, and today they are not distinguishable.

**Not fixed, and deliberately not.** The obvious lever is retracting `gamelog@1`,
which is a real state change to Sleven's store and reversing it is not something
I checked exists — so it is his call, not mine. The alternatives are dedup on the
observation's own identity (timestamp + line) rather than on the reading, or
counting DISTINCT events per fact alongside readings. Both are design decisions.

Flagging it now because an append-only store means an inflated count is not
something anybody removes later — the same argument that settled the ids.
