## Where we stand

    data snapshot            scunpacked  20260801T204744Z
    ships / components       316 / 3,283
    stamped                  LAST_VERIFIED_PATCH = "4.9"   (build_loadout_data.py:136)

**The site is not wrong. It is stamped.** Every page carries "last verified
against patch 4.9" in its own provenance line, and every row carries
`last_verified_patch`. That machinery was built for exactly today. Every
third-party Star Citizen tool is behind on patch day; the difference is whether
it says so. **Ours says so, on every row, without anybody doing anything.**

That is the standing architecture decision paying for itself, and it is worth
saying out loud before anybody panics: being a day behind is a known state, not
a silent lie.

## What is actually at risk

1. **Ships added in 4.10 are absent entirely.** The Kruger Stingray is named in
   the coverage; there may be more. A missing ship is the one failure the stamp
   does not cover, because a visitor searching for it sees nothing rather than
   a date.
2. **Fuel balance changed in 4.10** — flagged in the PTU notes back in July.
   Quantum range and fuel figures are the most likely numbers to be stale.
3. **New weapons and equipment** are missing from the parts catalogue, so a
   4.10 loadout cannot be built on our page yet.
4. **Already known and already written down:** 4.10 renamed the shop
   transaction class in `Game.log`
   (`CEntityComponentShopUIProvider::SendShopBuyRequest` became
   `CEntityComponentShoppingProvider::SendStandardItemBuyRequest`, plus a new
   `currencyType` field). `claude/FINDING_gamelog-is-a-data-source-4.9-vs-4.10.md`
   called this in advance and specified parsing on payload shape rather than on
   the emitting class name. **The collector should already survive it. Somebody
   should confirm that rather than assume it.**

## P2 — the refresh is not written down, and that is the real defect

The pieces all exist: `scripts/external_sources/scunpacked_com.py`,
`verify_snapshot.py`, `integrity_scan.py`, and the five gates Phase 1 built.
**What does not exist is a procedure that says "a patch dropped, do this."**

And `LAST_VERIFIED_PATCH` is a hard-coded constant in one Python file that
somebody has to remember to edit. On a project that assumes it still exists in
five years, "remember to edit a constant" is a defect, not a step.

**Write the runbook, as a script rather than as prose**: pull, gate, build,
stamp, diff, report. One command. The patch number is an argument to it, not a
line somebody edits.

## P3 — the diff report is the actual product

**This is the part worth caring about.** Nobody publishes a good answer to
"what changed between 4.9 and 4.10." Erkul shows you today's numbers. The wiki
shows you today's numbers. **We hold a sealed 4.9 snapshot and are about to
hold a sealed 4.10 one**, which means we can state the difference exactly:
ships added, ships removed, stats moved, components renamed, prices changed.

That is what `last_verified_patch` and the sealed-snapshot discipline were for.
It has never been used because there has never been a second snapshot to
compare against. **There is about to be.**

Build the diff as a real artifact — a data-layer output with a manifest, same
pattern as `location-gazetteer` and the rest — not a one-off script somebody
runs and pastes.

## Sequencing — and the recommendation

**Do not stop the viewer work for this.** The two lanes have different
bottlenecks:

- **Data lane** is blocked on a source that may not exist yet (P1). Mostly
  waiting, then mostly automated.
- **Viewer lane** is in flight, nearly done, and blocked on nothing.

V1 (the ship faces away) and V3 (the panel covers the hull) are minutes of work
and change the first impression of all 239 hulls. **Finish those first.** A
visitor arriving this week meets the picture before they meet the patch number.

There is also unconfirmed coverage suggesting a free-fly event around this
release. **I have not verified it and it is not a reason to rush anything** —
but if it is real, it is the week the most first-time visitors this project
will ever see are looking up ships, and the page they land on should show a
ship that looks like a ship.
