## 0. WHAT SLEVEN ASKED FOR, IN HIS WORDS

> "when the user clicks the ship name, it takes them to another page or another
> section directly for that particular ship they clicked on. They'll see the 3D
> model. They're able to mess with the components and the weapons and missiles to
> change the output... if they're trying to do a stealth build, there's a way for
> them to look at all the stealth components that we have and make it a lot more
> stealthier. But if they're doing stealthy, they also need to mess with the
> weapons and stuff so they know how much power they're actually able to put out."

**The reference is Erkul (erkul.games), confirmed by him.** Erkul does the
loadout maths. **It does not have the ship.** The 3D model with the hardpoints
shown in place is the thing Citizen Compass has and Erkul does not, and §J6 is
where that difference actually lands. Everything else here is table stakes.

**The tradeoff is the product.** A stealth build that cannot shoot is not a
build. Signature and weapons power must move **in the same view at the same
time**, or the page has not answered the question he asked it to answer.

## 2. RULINGS

**R9. THE LOADOUT BENCH BECOMES THE SHIP PAGE. Do not build a third page.**
`loadout.html` is already per-ship, already has A/B, already shares by URL. It is
missing the model. **Add the model to it** rather than creating a new page that
duplicates a calculator, or bolting a calculator into `index.html`, which is
already ~10,000 lines.
*Rejected - a new `ship.html`:* three places rendering ships instead of two.
*Rejected - merge into `index.html`:* makes the largest file larger and gives the
ship no URL of its own.

**R10. ONE 3D VIEWER IMPLEMENTATION, SHARED. Never a second copy.**
The viewer lives in `index.html` today. The ship page needs the same viewer.
**Two copies of a Three.js viewer is a guarantee of drift** - one gets a fix, the
other does not, and nobody notices for months. Extract it once and have both read
it. There are exactly two concrete consumers now, so this is not a premature
abstraction; it is the standing 2-3-cases rule being satisfied.

**R11. The 3D markers stay weapons-only. Do NOT re-litigate.** Physically visible
and mountable hardpoints - weapons, turrets, missile racks - get markers on the
hull. Internal components - power plant, cooler, shield, quantum drive - use the
menu overlay. That is settled; the ship page has two swap surfaces by design.

**R12. Missiles are checked now and BUILT LATER.** Sleven: *"I know we have
missiles... let's get as much put together and finished with what we just talked
about, and then we'll worry about coming back for missiles. But we'll get that
before it goes live."* So J1 measures and reports. **Nothing about missiles
blocks this run**, and it goes on the pre-live list at J10.

**J2. EXTRACT THE VIEWER, per R10.** One implementation, read by `index.html`

*Control:* the same ship rendered on both pages is **identical** - same model,
same markers, same count. Assert it, do not eyeball it.
*And the negative half:* break the shared module deliberately and confirm BOTH

**J3. THE SHIP PAGE.** Model and bench in one view. A person swaps a part and
**sees the numbers move without leaving, scrolling away, or losing the model.**
*Control:* swap a power plant; the model stays loaded and the stats change.

**J4. SIGNATURE IS FIRST-CLASS, not buried.** EM and IR sit beside DPS and power
draw, with stock-versus-current shown for each. **This is the stealth case and it
is the reason the page exists.** A person must be able to see, in one glance,
that the cooler that dropped their IR also cost them the power for a third gun.
*Control:* a build with lower IR and lower weapon output than stock shows BOTH
movements, in opposite directions, at the same time.

**J5. CLICK A HARDPOINT ON THE HULL, SWAP THAT WEAPON. This is the item that
makes the page not-Erkul.** A marker on the model selects the hardpoint it
belongs to; the picker opens for that mount, filtered by what `fits`.
*Control:* clicking marker N selects hardpoint N and no other. Assert by
identity, not by position on screen.
*If a ship has no hardpoint data* - and 25 do not - the page says so plainly and
the menu-driven swapping still works. **Never a spinner. Never an empty hull
presented as a complete one.**

**J6. THE SHIP NAME LINKS TO THE SHIP PAGE.** Not to RSI.
**The RSI link moves ONTO the ship page** and stays clearly available - it does
not disappear. Sleven's point: sending someone off-site the moment they click a
name means they never see what he built.
*Control:* every ship name in the list resolves to a ship page that loads.

**J7. THE SHARE LINK STILL WORKS.** `writeHash`/`readHash` already share a
build. It must survive the merge and now carry the ship as well as the parts.
*Control:* paste a shared URL into a clean session and get the same build back,
model and all.

**J8. PROVENANCE SURVIVES THE MERGE.** `loadout_data.gen.js` distinguishes
**CIG's own precomputed stock aggregate** from **anything the page sums from
parts**, and the page says which is which. **That distinction must not be lost

Same for `last_verified_patch` 4.9 and the 33 unreleased ships.
*Control:* a stat sourced from CIG and a stat computed by us are visibly
different on the rendered page.
