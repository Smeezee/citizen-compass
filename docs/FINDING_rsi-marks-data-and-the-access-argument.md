## 1. "FAN MADE" IS NOT WHAT §XIII.D IS ABOUT — a terminology trap

**"RSI Fansite Content" means CIG's OWN images, graphics and artwork, licensed
for use on a fansite. It does NOT mean fan-made material.**

The distinction matters because it reverses the risk:

    CIG's artwork          §XIII.D governs it. Granted for a free, ad-free
                           fansite. Restricted the moment you charge or take
                           ad/sponsor money.
    Fan-MADE artwork       §XIII.D says NOTHING about it. It belongs to the
                           fan who made it. Permission comes from that person,
                           not from CIG, and being free does not help.

**This is exactly the `cstone.space` problem.** The 4,805 item images found in
the wiki source are hosted by Cornerstone. **CIG granting fansite use of CIG's
art has no bearing on somebody else's copies. Two separate permissions, and we
hold neither.**

---

## 2. THE "ACCESS, NOT INFORMATION" DISTINCTION IS NOT IN THE TERMS

Proposed reading: *the website is free, the subscription buys access to the AI,
therefore the fansite is not charged for.*

**That distinction does not appear anywhere in §XIII.D and should not be relied
on.** The clause bars using RSI Fansite Content and Marks *"on your fansite if
you charge a subscription or access fee to access your fansite."*

**Nothing in the terms defines where one property ends and another begins.** A
paid assistant that is fed by Citizen Compass's data, branded alongside it, and
promoted from it could reasonably be read as one property with a paywalled tier.
**C2 cannot say how CIG would read it, and neither can Sleven. It is untested.**

**The safe formulation does not depend on that argument at all:**

> You may charge for a product that uses none of CIG's images, artwork,
> trademarks or logos.

**That is what the sentence actually restricts. Build to it and the boundary
question never has to be answered.**

---

## 3. THE HARD PART NOBODY HAS NAMED — "RSI Marks"

§XIII.D restricts **"RSI Fansite Content and/or RSI Marks"**. C2's earlier note
treated the marks half as trivially avoidable. **It is not.**

**Ship names and manufacturer names are almost certainly trademarks** — Drake,
Aegis, Anvil, Origin, Vulture, Carrack, Idris, and "Star Citizen" itself.

**A Star Citizen assistant cannot function without naming ships and
manufacturers.** So "use no RSI Marks" is not a design choice that can simply be
taken; it is structurally impossible for this product.

**The doctrine that normally covers this is nominative use** — using a
trademark to refer to the actual thing it names, where there is no other
reasonable way to identify it, no more of the mark than necessary is used, and
nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement. **That is a general legal principle
and a defence, not a permission granted by these terms, and C2 is not qualified
to say how far it reaches.**

**Practical consequences that follow regardless:**

    use the WORDS, never the LOGOS      wordmarks in running text are a very
                                        different thing from reproducing
                                        manufacturer logo artwork
    no CIG artwork anywhere in a paid product
    a prominent, unmissable "not affiliated with or endorsed by Cloud Imperium"
    do not style the product to look like an official RSI property

**This is the single largest unexamined risk in the Historian's business model,
and it was not on any list before tonight.**

---

## 4. AND THE CLAUSE SAYS NOTHING ABOUT THE DATA AT ALL

**§XIII.D grants images, graphics, artwork, trademarks and logos. It does not
mention game data, statistics, item records or extracted files — in either
direction.**

So the material both projects actually run on — 21,849 item records, 316 ships,
5,108 contracts, 206 commodities, extracted from the client — **is not licensed
by this clause, and is not restricted by it either. It is simply not addressed.**

**What carries it today is the assumption that facts and numbers are not
copyrightable.** That is broadly right in US law for raw data. **The boundary
around a *selection and arrangement* of data is genuinely contested, and nobody
in this project has examined it.**

**Being free does not settle it and neither does charging.** It is a separate
question from §XIII.D entirely, and it applies to Citizen Compass exactly as
much as to the Historian.
