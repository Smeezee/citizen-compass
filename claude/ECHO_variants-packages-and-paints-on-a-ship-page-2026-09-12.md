Echo — a design and a research question, from Sleven. Labels as usual: ESTABLISHED, RECOMMENDATION, FORECAST.

## THE SITE, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Citizen Compass is a free fan-made Star Citizen reference site, plain files, no accounts. Its front page is a searchable list of 253 ship cards with prices and where to buy them in the game. Clicking a card opens that ship's own page.

## THE PROBLEM

A ship in Star Citizen exists in several shapes, and our list currently treats them all as separate cards:

- **Editions**: the same ship with a different paint or trim — a Gladius Pirate, a Constellation Phoenix Emerald.
- **Packages**: several different ships sold together as one purchase — the "Carrack Expedition" is a Carrack, a Pisces and an Ursa, all three of which we already list separately.
- **Real variants**: a different machine with different capability — a Cutlass Black against a Cutlass Red.

Sleven has ruled the rule for editions: cosmetic or paint-only folds under the base ship; physically or functionally different gets its own card. A package is neither — it is a bundle of ships we already hold.

## WHAT HE WANTS, IN HIS OWN WORDS

"The way I'm envisioning it is kind of like how Star Citizen does it. They kind of should tell you, hey, there's variants to this ship, and the user can click on it, and it will tell them this one comes with the Pisces, or this one comes with the Pisces and the ground vehicle. And by clicking on that, they can still see that it's the ship, there's no difference, but there is a link that takes them to the corresponding variant RSI link. I think that should kind of be how it is for everything. So if somebody likes a paint job, they can click on the paint that they like, and it takes them to the corresponding URL at RSI, until we can figure out how to actually put the paints on the ship."

So: one card per real ship, with a list of its shapes on the page — each one saying plainly what it is or what it includes, each one linking out to that exact thing on the official store.

## WHAT WE MEASURED TODAY, BECAUSE IT CONSTRAINS THE ANSWER

Counted by machine over all 253 rows, 2026-09-12:

- 24 rows have **no link to the official store at all** — and most of them are exactly the edition rows this design depends on: 600i Executive Edition, Carrack Expedition, Constellation Phoenix Emerald, Gladius Pirate, Gladius Dunlevy, Mustang Omega, Sabre Raven, Ursa Fortuna, P-72 Archimedes Emerald, F8C Lightning Executive Edition, the Hornet Heartseeker rows, and others.
- 217 of 253 ships have a 3D model on their page. 195 also have weapon-mount markers on it.
- No ship has a width or a height recorded. Length only.
- Every ship has a picture except six.

**So the link this design sends people to does not exist yet for the rows that need it most, and finding those links is work somebody has to do.**

## QUESTION ONE — HOW THIS SHOULD BE PRESENTED

Not our layout preferences. What is known to work, and what is known to fail, for showing "this thing comes in these shapes" on a catalogue page.

1. Is there established practice or evidence for how to show variants of one product without making a visitor think they are different products? Retail catalogues, car configurators, parts catalogues, game wikis — wherever it has actually been studied.
2. A package is a bundle that contains other items we also list on their own. Is there a known pattern for showing "this purchase contains these three things you can also look at separately", without it reading as a fourth product?
3. Where should it sit on the page — inline near the ship's name, its own section, or behind a control the visitor opens?
4. Our honest constraint: we do not track what packages cost and do not intend to. Is there evidence on how a list of purchasable shapes with no prices is read — helpful, or frustrating?
5. Search. Today someone searching "Carrack Expedition" finds a card. If that card becomes a line on the Carrack page, the words still have to be findable. What do sites that collapse variants do about search?

## QUESTION TWO — PAINT ON THE MODEL

Sleven wants to know whether we can ever show a paint scheme on the ship model itself, rather than only linking out to the store page for it.

What we have: a 3D model per ship, shown in a web page. What we do not have is any paint or livery data.

- Does Star Citizen publish paint or livery information anywhere a fan site could legitimately use — in the game files, through any published data, or through any community dataset that is maintained rather than abandoned?
- If the paint data does not exist, is there an accepted way fan sites handle this — an official image per paint, a colour swatch, a tinted render, or simply a name and a link?
- Anything about the rights position on showing a game's own artwork this way would help. We publish under CIG's Fan Kit terms, non-commercially, and we do not guess on rights.
