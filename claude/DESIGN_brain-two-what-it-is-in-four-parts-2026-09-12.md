**I ruled "an auditor that flags and never fixes" and repeated it three times. That is
deliberately less than what he asked for, and I never said why in terms of what he wanted
— I said it in terms of what I was afraid of.**

**The self-heal idea is four separate capabilities. I judged all four by the risk of the
riskiest one.**

    1  FIND      notice what is missing, wrong, stale or contradictory
    2  FIX       repair what can be repaired from what we ALREADY HOLD
    3  FETCH     go out and get what we do not hold
    4  ASK       put a question to the whole record and get an answer

**Only 3 carries the risk I kept naming. 1, 2 and 4 carry none of it.**

# 2. FIX FROM WHAT WE ALREADY HOLD — WORTH BUILDING, AND IT TOUCHES NO NETWORK

**This is the half I never separated out, and it is most of what "self-healing" actually
means.**

    a document exists only in the project store   copy it to disk
    an entry's DONE-WHEN is satisfied             write the disposition
    a count has no surface named                  recompute it from the data
                                                  and name the surface
    a letter closed with no ANSWERS marker        add the marker
    a citation points at a moved file             repoint it

**None of that needs the open web. Every input is already on his machine.** So the
injection surface I kept citing does not exist for this half at all.

**It proposes a batch and applies it on one word — not silently, and never on its own
timer.** That is the difference between a repair and the autonomy rule.

# 3. FETCH — WE ALREADY HAVE THIS, AND OURS IS BETTER THAN THE SKILL'S

**The "go and research the missing information" half is CIC, and it has been running for
weeks.**

**And it carries rules the skill does not:** primary source read to the end, the maker's
own words before any wiki or aggregator, verbatim quotes with the URL each was read from,
reconcile a sweep against a known list, and — added tonight — **one row whose answer is
known from outside the sweep.**

**The skill's version is a worse instance of a desk we already run.** Adopting it would be
a second research system with weaker rules.

**SO THE GAP IS NOT RESEARCH. IT IS ROUTING.** Nothing today turns *"the auditor found a
hole"* into *"a letter in Research's tray."* **That connector is small and it is the piece
that makes the other three add up to the thing he is describing.**

    auditor finds a gap  ->  files a letter to the desk that can close it
    desk answers          ->  comes back through the mail as it already does
    C1 rules              ->  record updated

**That is "Brain 2.0". Every piece except the auditor and the router already exists.**
