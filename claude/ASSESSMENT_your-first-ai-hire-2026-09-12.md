# WHAT IT IS

**A beginner's guide to installing Claude Code and handing off one real task.** Make a
folder, install it, write a `CLAUDE.md`, give it a job, walk away, come back to finished
work. Six steps, about thirty minutes, explicitly for people with no technical background.

**Its whole thesis in its own line:** *"The old game was hard work. The new game is smart
decisions."*

# THE SCORES

    as a guide for HIM                              2 / 10
    as something to hand to somebody else            8 / 10
    as a source of ideas for this project            1 / 10

**2 is not a criticism of the writing. He is roughly eighteen months past its ending.**
Its climax — write a `CLAUDE.md` and hand off one task — is where he started. He now runs
six desks, a mail system, a watcher service, a sweep of a hundred-plus controls, a written
doctrine and a rules file of twenty-six numbered rules with an incident behind each one.

**8 for handing on, and that is a real 8.** It is concrete, every step puts something on
the screen, and it is honest about the part most guides skip — that walking away feels
wrong the first time. **For anyone stuck at "I open a chat, feel busy, and close the
tab", it is a good thirty minutes.**

---

# THE ONE LINE WORTH KEEPING

> *"Add a rule only when you catch your AI making the same mistake twice."*

**That is the discipline `CLAUDE.md` already follows here and it is worth having stated
from outside.** Every rule in that file was paid for by an incident.

**And it is the direct counter to the advice he read about wiping a rules file every six
months** — the argument the rules-file-split proposal already makes, now with an
independent voice behind it. **A rules file that only grows by incident is not clutter; it
is receipts.**

Two lesser ones, already true here: *describe the outcome, not the steps*, and *fix the
brief, not your patience*.

---

# WHERE IT IS WRONG OR THIN, FOR THE RECORD

**Version control as an "optional safety net."** For anything touching real files that is
underselling it badly. For the writing task it uses as its example, fair enough.

**"Paste the error and ask the AI itself."** Fine for an install. A bad habit to carry
into anything that matters.

**It has no answer for the thing that actually breaks at scale** — what happens when a
decision made in one handoff needs to be known by the next one. **That is the entire
problem this project has spent a month on: the record, the trays, two `claude/` folders,
twelve documents in the wrong store.** The guide stops one step before that wall exists.
